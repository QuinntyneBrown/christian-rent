Design system

christian.rent design system

The visual and interaction language of christian.rent, written down once: the tokens, the foundations, every component with its states, and the patterns that join them. It is extracted from the mocks, which are the source of truth for the UI.

Components

One page per component, each with the same thirteen sections. The component is a BEM block; its class names are the ones the mocks and the e2e locators use, and each becomes one presentation component (cr-*) with its own encapsulated stylesheet.

Brand

LogoThe mark, a key whose head is a cross, and the lowercase wordmark with “.rent” in orange.

Actions

ButtonA button does something: it posts, sends, approves, removes. Its colour says what kind of thing it does.LinkA link goes somewhere and changes nothing.

Inputs

Text fieldOne line of text in a bordered box: a name, a church, a city.TextareaSeveral lines of a member’s own words: a message, a listing’s note, a report’s note.SelectOne choice from a list that is too long to lay out: a member’s church, the period a price is per.CheckboxOne thing a person agrees to: the declaration that they are a Christian.Radio groupOne choice from a short fixed list: the reason for a report.Date rangeA start and an end date, sent with a message about a listing for rent or given as a listing’s “when”.Form fieldThe wrapper every input sits in: its label, then the control, then help, an error or a counter.Line fieldPost’s big field: a question, and an answer written large on a single line.Word toggleThe highlighted words in Post’s sentence: “have” or “need”, “a space” or “a thing”.Photo pickerAdds up to six photos to a listing for rent, and shows which one goes on the card.Search barBrowse’s search: what you are looking for, where, and Search.Kind filterNarrows Browse to spaces or to things.

Navigation

Switch (Everything, For rent, Wanted)Browse’s switch between everything, what is for rent and what is wanted.Site headerThe bar at the top of Browse and Listing: the logo, who is signed in, and Post.Admin navigationThe ink rail in Admin: the logo, who the admin is, the review queue with its count, the way back to the site, and Sign out.Sign inThe sign-in form of either application: email, password, Sign in, and who creates accounts.Admin gateAdmin’s ink page around one card: its sign-in and its forbidden page.Skip linkThe first thing the Tab key reaches: a link past the header to the content.

Containers and overlays

DialogA short task over the page: report a listing, confirm Remove, become a member.Message boxThe panel beside a listing that sends a message to the person who posted it.Review detailAdmin’s detail panel: everything needed to decide on one item, and the buttons that decide.Browse headlineThe top of Browse: what christian.rent is, and the two buttons that lead straight to posting.Layout primitivesThe container, the stack and the cluster that every screen is laid out with.

Data display

Listing cardOne listing on Browse: a photo for something for rent, a blue card with no photo for something wanted.Photo galleryA listing’s photos: one large, the rest as thumbnails to step through.Wanted panelThe head of a wanted listing: where a photo would be, the request itself in white on blue.Posted byWho posted a listing and the church they belong to, each marked Verified when an admin has confirmed it.Queue itemOne thing waiting in Admin’s review queue: a new listing, a report or a new member.AvatarA member’s initials on a pale tile.TagA short label: what kind of listing, or what kind of item in the queue.Verified markAn icon and the word “Verified” beside a member or a church an admin has confirmed.

How to use

  • Link tokens/tokens.css, then assets/components.css. The mocks in docs/mocks do exactly this.
  • Use the classes as documented. A state is an attribute (aria-current, aria-invalid, aria-pressed, aria-busy, disabled); data-state exists only so these pages can show hover and focus without a pointer.
  • For another platform, consume tokens/tokens.json.
  • When a mock changes, change the system with it: the mocks lead.

christian.rent is two applications of a few screens and narrow on purpose, so this system documents the components those screens use and no others. It has no table, tabs, menu, tooltip, breadcrumb or pagination, because no screen has one.
