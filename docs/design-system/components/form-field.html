Form field

The wrapper every input sits in: its label, then the control, then help, an error or a counter.

Overview

Every input on christian.rent is inside a form field, so labels, help and errors are always in the same place. The error summary that heads an invalid form is part of this component; how a whole form behaves is the forms pattern.

Anatomy

  1. LabelSemibold 14 px. “Optional” is written beside it; nothing is marked with an asterisk.
  2. ControlA text field, textarea, select or date.
  3. Help, error or counterOne line under the control. An error replaces the help.

With help, and optional with a counter

Responsive behaviour

field-row puts two fields side by side when there is room: from 576 px to 767 px and again from 992 px on Post, and from 576 px in a dialog.

Theming

Form field reads only semantic tokens, so it follows the theme with no rule of its own. The colour tokens it reads are --color-fg-default, --color-fg-subtle, --color-fg-muted, --color-danger-fg, --color-danger-solid. To restyle it, change those tokens; never write a colour in the component.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A <label> wrapping its control, or tied with for.

Keyboard

KeyAction
NoneNot focusable: it is read in document order.

Focus

The shared focus ring: a 2 px outline in --color-focus-ring, offset 2 px, never removed.

Labelling

Help and errors are joined to the control with aria-describedby. The summary has role="alert", receives focus on submit, and each line links to its field.

Motion

Colour and position changes take --duration-fast or --duration-base with --ease-standard. With reduced motion the tokens drop to 0.01 ms, so nothing moves.

Touch

Every interactive part is at least 44 × 44 px (--target-min). Nothing depends on hover.

Content

  • An error starts with what to do: “Enter a city or neighbourhood in Canada, such as ‘Willoughby, Langley’.”
  • It gives the rule and the number: “A title is 3 to 80 characters. This one is 2.”
  • The summary counts: “3 things need fixing before this can be posted.”
